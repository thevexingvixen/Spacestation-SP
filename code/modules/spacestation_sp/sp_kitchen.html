/tg/ Station 13 - Modules - Types

code/modules/spacestation_sp/sp_kitchen.dm

/proc/sp_kitchen_areasSpacestation SP — the kitchen.
/proc/sp_kitchen_turfsEvery turf of the kitchen on this z-level.
/proc/sp_in_kitchenTRUE if this atom is standing in the kitchen.
/proc/sp_find_prep_tableThe table the chef prepares food on: the kitchen table with the most cooking machinery around it.
/proc/sp_find_counterThe kitchen's counter.
/proc/sp_find_service_counterThe counter of a service department: one of its tables with an open tile on the other side that is not part of the department. That is the serving hatch on every map that has one — on MetaStation it picks out exactly the row of kitchen tables the bar stools face, and the bar's own counter likewise. Falls back to any table in the department, so a map without a hatch still gets somewhere to put things.
/proc/sp_find_kitchen_machineThe nearest working machine of this type inside the kitchen.
/proc/sp_pantry_contentsEverything piled on the prep table, which is what the crafting code will see from beside it.
/proc/sp_pantry_countHow many things of this type are on the prep table already.
/datum/sp_prep_stepOne rung of the cooking tree: an ingredient, what you do to it, and what you get.
/proc/sp_find_prep_stepPicks the next prep step: a rung of the tree we can climb, and the ingredient on the table to climb it with. Returns list(step, item), or null when there is nothing worth doing.
/proc/sp_bakes_into_somethingWould the oven turn this into something better than it is now?
/proc/sp_grills_into_somethingThe same question for the griddle.
/proc/sp_prep_step_appliesGuards the rungs whose applicability is a property of the item rather than its type. The component is dropped once something is cooked, so these also answer "is this still raw?".
/proc/sp_machine_roomHow many more things this machine will take on one trip, and whether it should be disturbed at all.
/proc/sp_prep_batchEverything else on the table this same step would take, up to what the machine will hold.
/proc/sp_kitchen_toolA kitchen tool of this type the chef is carrying.
/datum/sp_kitchen_mixSomething made by pouring reagents into a bowl. /tg/ handles these as chemical reactions rather than crafting recipes, so they are the one part of the tree the crafting code cannot reach — and without them the chef has no dough and no cheese, which means no bread, no buns and no sandwiches.
/proc/sp_find_mixing_bowlA bowl the chef can mix in: empty, and either carried or sitting on the prep table.
/proc/sp_find_reagent_sourceA container within reach holding at least amount of this reagent.
/proc/sp_find_mixPicks a mix worth making. Water is deliberately not searched for: the kitchen sink is the only sensible source and the chef has to walk to it, which the behaviour tree handles as its own leg.
/proc/sp_find_kitchen_sinkThe nearest kitchen sink, for the water half of a mix.
/proc/sp_cooking_recipesEvery food recipe anyone can craft without having read a cookbook first, built once.
/proc/sp_craftable_recipesEverything the chef could craft standing where they are. This runs /tg/'s own checks, so if it says yes the craft will go through.
/proc/sp_recipe_makes_dishWould this recipe produce something the crew could eat, rather than another component?
/proc/sp_is_finished_dishA dish worth carrying out to the counter: something cooked, that is not itself an ingredient in one of the chef's prep steps, and that the game does not still consider raw.
/proc/sp_find_dish_to_serveA finished dish the chef is carrying, standing on, or has left within arm's reach.
/proc/sp_counter_loadHow much is already waiting on the counter, so the chef stops before burying it.
/proc/sp_is_ingredientTRUE when this is an ingredient the chef would carry to the prep table.
/proc/sp_find_stock_sourceWhere the next armful of ingredients is coming from: a fridge or cabinet with food in it, or food somebody left lying around the kitchen, which is how botany's deliveries arrive.
/proc/sp_worth_openingIs this fridge or cabinet worth a trip?
/proc/sp_find_bowl_vendorThe dinnerware vendor, when the kitchen is short of bowls. A great many recipes want one and each is consumed by the dish it goes into, so a chef who cannot restock them runs out of half the menu after a few plates.
/proc/sp_buy_bowlBuys one bowl out of the chef's own wages, the way the botanist buys seeds.
/proc/sp_stock_inThe ingredients inside a fridge, cabinet or smartfridge.
/proc/sp_find_finished_machineA cooking machine in the kitchen holding something that has finished cooking.
/proc/sp_finished_in_machineWhat inside a griddle or oven is cooked and ready to come out.
