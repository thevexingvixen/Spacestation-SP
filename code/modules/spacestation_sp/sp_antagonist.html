/datum/sp_schemeThe antagonist foundation.
/datum/sp_scheme/stealGet hold of one particular item and hang on to it. The target comes from TG's own steal-objective catalogue (/datum/objective_item), so it is a thing the game already knows is worth stealing and confirms is on the map — the captain's medal, the CMO's hypospray — without taking on the objective or uplink machinery around it.
/proc/sp_nearest_steal_itemThe nearest instance of target_type on the pawn's z-level that is out in the world (not already in somebody's pockets). Uses TG's steal-item handler, which tracks every registered instance on the station, so it finds the item wherever it was mapped without an oview sweep.
/proc/sp_can_be_liftedWhether a thief could actually walk up to this and take it: lying out in the open, or shut in a closet we can open. Not something nested in a box, and not something being worn or carried.
/proc/sp_issue_uplink_kitWhat a thief draws from the uplink before setting out: a doorjack, which hacks airlocks open and recharges, and an emag, which breaks the lock on any secure closet. TG keeps the two apart -- the plain emag refuses every airlock type (type_blacklist), the doorjack refuses everything but doors -- so a thief needs both. These are the missing half of the theft funnel measured in docs/02 §8: every target was behind a door. Both are contraband, so an arrest and a search take them back (sp_search.dm).
/proc/sp_doorjack_carriedThe doorjack somebody carries, with a charge left on it, or null.
/proc/sp_emag_carriedThe emag somebody carries, or null. A doorjack is an emag by type but opens no lockers, so it does not count.
/proc/sp_thief_accessThe access the kit gives a thief: their own, and every station door while they carry a charged doorjack. This is what a target is picked against (sp_reachable_steal_item()), and what the pathfinder gets on the walk to it, and only on that walk (/datum/ai_controller/sp_crew/antagonist/get_access()).
/datum/sp_walk_planHow a thief would walk to something: the access the walk is planned with, and how many doors on the way their own card will not open.
/proc/sp_scheme_walk_planPlans the walk to reach: the route, as the pathfinder would take it, and how many doors on it need the doorjack. The corridors are tried before the tunnels (every station access but maintenance, then everything), because a maintenance route is a locked door every few tiles: the first thief to walk with every door open went from cargo to medbay through the tunnels, four hacks for a trip the corridors do in two, and stalled flat at the fourth. A route that needs no hack is walked with the thief's own card, so any re-path on the way stays on doors that open for them. Returns null when nothing reaches. Sleeps: it asks the pathfinder.
/proc/sp_hacks_on_pathDoors on a planned path that this thief's own card will not open: airlocks and windoors shut to them, hacked ones aside.
/proc/sp_doorjack_ownedThe doorjack somebody carries, charged or flat, or null.
/proc/sp_jackable_doorA door the doorjack works on: an airlock or a windoor. Firelocks are pushed open by anybody (on_bump()).
/proc/sp_jack_doorHacks a door open with the doorjack, as the thief walks into it. The hack itself is TG's: the door opens 0.6 seconds on and stays open for good. Done once per door at a time (BB_SP_JACKING_DOOR), since a walk bumps the same door several times a second. Somebody may see it. Sleeps for the click.
/proc/sp_break_lockBreaks the lock on a secure closet with the emag. Sleeps for the click. Somebody may see it.
/proc/sp_reachable_steal_itemPicks a steal target for an antagonist: an on-the-map, normal-type catalogue item this crew member is not the owner of, that actually has a locatable instance right now. Returns the /datum/objective_item, or null if nothing suitable exists (an empty test map, say).
/proc/sp_make_thiefGives controller a scheme to steal a chosen (or picked) catalogue item. Returns the scheme, or null.
